        <p>This study aims to explore and quantify the scope of research applying cross-linguistic corpus linguistic methods to the investigation of phonetic and phonological phenomena. By conducting a bibliometric analysis of publications from 2010 to 2020, the study seeks to identify key trends, influential contributors, and emerging topics, thereby providing insight into the evolution and impact of research within this field.</p>

        <h4>Methodological Framework</h4>
        <ul>
            <li>Data collection from the Scopus database using a multifaceted search query</li>
            <li>Two-stage screening process (title screening followed by abstract review)</li>
            <li>Annotation of corpus types (written, spoken, both, sign language) and languages covered</li>
            <li>Mapping of research streams via quantitative bibliometric indicators</li>
        </ul>

        <h4>Key Findings</h4>
        <div class="row">
            <div class="col-sm-6">
                <h5>Corpus Types</h5>
                <ul>
                    <li>Written corpora: 823 instances</li>
                    <li>Spoken corpora: 547 instances</li>
                    <li>Both written and spoken: 92 instances</li>
                    <li>Sign language corpora: 19 instances</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
            <div class="col-sm-6">
                <h5>Languages Covered</h5>
                <ul>
                    <li>Total languages identified: 212</li>
                    <li>English (and its variants): 505 occurrences</li>
                    <li>Spanish: 164 occurrences</li>
                    <li>French: 84 occurrences</li>
                    <li>German: 71 occurrences</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

        <h4 class="mt-4">Conclusion</h4>
        <p>This bibliometric analysis highlights the evolving landscape of corpus linguistic research. The findings illuminate the extensive use of written data, the rich diversity of languages examined, and the emergence of distinct research streams that collectively inform current scholarly practices and future research directions in the field. The study underscores the value of quantitative methods in revealing research trends and identifying underexplored areas, paving the way for innovative inquiries in linguistic research.</p>
